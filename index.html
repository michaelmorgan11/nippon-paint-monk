The exceptions you named, and what Monk does with them

Short pay, no reason given

The remittance tells you which invoice. It does not tell you which part, or why. The variance sits on the balance sheet until someone works out whether it is pricing, an RGA, or a credit memo.

Monk: matches the payment, sizes the shortfall against the invoice, and triggers a playbook to the customer’s AP within hours asking for the line item and reason — then routes it to sales with the detail attached.
Bounced or stale AP contact

Sales only knows procurement. The AP contact changes and nobody hears about it, so outreach looks covered but never happened.

Monk: detects the bounce or departure reply, queries Apollo and ZoomInfo, writes the new contact into the customer record, and resumes the thread without a gap.
Portal rejection on Toyota or Honda

Roughly 40 of 500 invoices and a similar share of revenue. A rejection sends no notification back, so the clock starts late on the two largest accounts.

Monk: scans both connections on a schedule, double-attaches PO and supporting docs to reduce rejection odds, and works the error the day it appears.
Bill of lading required before payment

One customer requires proof of delivery alongside the invoice. Today operations forwards it to billing and billing forwards it on.

Monk: anything emailed to the connected inbox is attached to the right invoice and sent with the next touch, with no one on your team in the middle.
